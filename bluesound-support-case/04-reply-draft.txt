Hi Charmain,

The network, question by question.

Router: an Orange Pi 5 Plus (16 GB) single-board computer running Ubuntu, doing
the routing and firewalling. Not a consumer router.

Switches: a Ubiquiti EdgeSwitch 24 Lite, firmware 1.9.3, in the office; a
MikroTik CSS106-5G-1S running SwOS 2.18 in the server closet, which is the main
switch; and an HP 1410-8G in the media rack. No bridges.

Access points: a TP-Link EAP772, hardware 1.0, firmware 1.1.5, in the office —
this is the one the wireless player uses; and a Ubiquiti UniFi AP AC Pro in the
server closet, on its own SSID, to extend coverage. I have used the second one
as a comparison during testing.

Ethernet Power Line Adapters: none. Nothing on my network carries Ethernet over
mains wiring.

How the players and controllers connect: three of the four players are wired,
one on each of the three switches, and the fourth is on Wi-Fi via the EAP772.
The three wired players behave identically despite sitting behind three
different switches. The controllers are the part I have varied deliberately, as
in my earlier messages — the Android phones over Wi-Fi, over USB Ethernet with
the radio still enabled, and over USB Ethernet with the radio switched off; the
iPhone over Wi-Fi and separately over a cable; two laptops over Wi-Fi
throughout; and one virtualised Android instance wired. Every measurement I
quote below was taken with the controller on the players' own VLAN, reaching
them directly.

Ethernet cable type: Cat 5e throughout, in mostly short runs of a few metres
— my longest patch cable is 10 m. The exception is the in-wall run between the
EdgeSwitch in the office and the MikroTik in the server closet, which is Cat 6a
and at most about 12 m. Cabling is one of the things I have unintentionally
tested hardest: the problem has followed me across many different cables, and
they were thoroughly reshuffled when I moved house. No cable I have swapped has
ever made a difference to it.

Wireless distances: the wireless player is about 2.5 m from the EAP772 with one
wall in between, and its signal is stable. When testing with a phone I stand
between the two access points, 2-5 m from either, so the distance is comfortable
whichever one the phone is on.

Two things that may save you time.

The wireless player is not the bottleneck, and the measurement shows that in the
most useful way: you cannot tell from the numbers which player it is. I measured
LSDP discovery directly, from a wired machine on the players' VLAN — 20 rounds,
each sending the same discovery query a controller sends and recording when each
player answered. All four players answered in all 20 rounds. Median time to
answer, one line per player:

  327 ms
  362 ms
  461 ms
  468 ms

One of those four is the player on Wi-Fi. It is not the slowest of them, and
nothing in the spread marks it out. Every answer fell inside the random 0-750 ms
a BluOS player waits before answering a query, and no round needed a repeat
query, so nothing was lost anywhere. Its wireless link is adding neither delay
nor loss, and it is not what the Android app is waiting for.

And on where the remaining time goes. The only change that removes the problem
on Android is switching the Wi-Fi radio off. With a USB Ethernet adapter
attached and the radio left enabled, nothing improves: about three seconds, and
still sometimes short of all four players. The same adapter with the radio
switched off gives about one second, complete on every single run. Traffic goes
over the cable in both cases — only the radio's state differs. So whatever is
costing the time keys on whether the Wi-Fi radio is enabled rather than on which
interface is carrying discovery traffic, and that points at the controller
rather than at anything on my network. The iPhone, on the same access point and
the same VLAN, shows no penalty on either link.

I am glad to run anything else you would like measured.
