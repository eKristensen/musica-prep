Hi Charmain,

The network, question by question.

Router: an Orange Pi single-board computer running Ubuntu, doing the routing
and firewalling. Not a consumer router.

Switches and access points: a Ubiquiti EdgeSwitch and a MikroTik switch, a
TP-Link access point and a Ubiquiti access point. No bridges. Say the word if
you want exact models and firmware versions.

Ethernet Power Line Adapters: none. Nothing on my network carries Ethernet
over mains wiring.

How the players and controllers connect: three of the four players are wired,
the fourth is on Wi-Fi. The controllers are the part I have varied
deliberately, as in my earlier messages — the Android phones over Wi-Fi, over
USB Ethernet with the radio still on, and over USB Ethernet with the radio
switched off; the iPhone over Wi-Fi and separately over a cable; two laptops
over Wi-Fi throughout; and one virtualised Android instance wired. Every
measurement I have quoted was taken with the controller on the players' own
VLAN, reaching them directly.

Ethernet cable type: Cat 5e throughout.

Wireless distances: the player that is on Wi-Fi is 1-2 m from the TP-Link
access point, on 2.4 GHz. The phones under test were a few metres from an
access point.

Two things that may save you time.

First, the network side can be closed out by a control I have already run. An
iPhone and an Android phone, on the same Wi-Fi, through the same access point,
on the same VLAN, to the same four players: the iPhone lists all four at once,
every time, and the Android phone takes 3-5 seconds and comes back short of at
least one player about one attempt in five. Only the operating system differs.
Separately, an LSDP discovery query from a wired machine on the players' VLAN
gets answers from all four players within 0.75 seconds, twenty runs out of
twenty. So the players and the segment answer promptly even at the moments the
Android app is showing nothing.

Second, I believe I have found what accounts for the Android numbers, and it is
in the app rather than on my network. Discovery is delayed by a flat two seconds
whenever the Wi-Fi radio is enabled, regardless of which interface is actually
carrying the traffic. The delay sits around acquiring the Wi-Fi multicast lock,
so it is skipped entirely when the radio is off — which is exactly the split I
measured: about a second and complete every time with the radio off, against
3-5 seconds and sometimes incomplete with it on, cable attached or not. I read
this out of the shipping Android app; the discovery code is byte-identical
between 4.16.2 and 4.16.3, so it applies to both.

I would be glad to write that up in as much detail as whoever works on the
Android controller finds useful. Is that something you are able to pass on?
